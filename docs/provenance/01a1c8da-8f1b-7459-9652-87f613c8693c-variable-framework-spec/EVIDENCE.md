# Evidence

Research and measurements behind the variable/condition framework spec: cohorts, suite splits, conditional visibility and form variables. Gathered 2026-11-10 to 12 by research subagents, lab runs and prototypes.

**Source status** follows root `AGENTS.md` **Established practice**: formal standard, vendor documentation, research (published empirical study), or common practice. Legal sources say which kind they are: a statute or regulation is a formal standard, a court ruling is binding case law, and regulator guidance is official guidance. **Search snippet** means the researcher saw only a search-result snippet. **Unverified** means the page could not be fetched or the claim was not confirmed.

**Repository and data.** Code facts were read at `9a1bd16140` (inventory, critic, design and placement work) or at `81af37d5a0` (previous-answer, errors and launch-gate work, and the code as of this file). Line numbers may drift between the two commits. Data comes from `alliance_variable_framework`, a staging copy: production with personal data removed, loaded 2026-11-10. Every query was a SELECT, mostly with `default_transaction_read_only=on`. Results are aggregates; no member identities appear here.

## External research

Every finding below was checked 2026-11-10 unless its section says otherwise.

### How do audience-targeting products split an audience into groups?

- LaunchDarkly evaluates individual targets first, then rules top to bottom (first match wins), then a fallthrough default. Conditions within a rule are ANDed. [Flag evaluation rules](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/sdk/concepts/flag-evaluation-rules), [flag hierarchy](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/guides/flags/flag-hierarchy). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- LaunchDarkly skips a rule that references a null or absent attribute, even under a negated operator. [Custom rules](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/flags/custom-rules). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- LaunchDarkly experiments in one layer never share traffic, and separate layers overlap. Buckets come from a hash of the key plus a per-iteration seed. [Mutually exclusive](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/experimentation/mutually-exclusive), [reshuffle](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/experimentation/reshuffle). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10. LaunchDarkly's own pages conflict on whether reshuffling is the default.
- GrowthBook evaluates rules top to bottom, first match wins, with a default when none match. Namespaces with non-overlapping 0–1 ranges are mutually exclusive. Sticky bucketing is opt-in and new settings reach only users not yet bucketed. [Rules](https://docs.growthbook.io/features/rules), [namespaces](https://docs.growthbook.io/feature-flag-experiments), [sticky bucketing](https://docs.growthbook.io/app/sticky-bucketing). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Statsig evaluates rules top to bottom with first match. Within a layer, an unallocated user gets the layer defaults, and a user who fails one experiment's targeting is not moved to another. That is a different procedure from a first-match list. [Create](https://docs.statsig.com/feature-flags/create), [layers](https://docs.statsig.com/experiments/layers-overview). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Optimizely rules run top to bottom and end in "Then, for everyone else". Exclusion groups are for use "only when required", with membership left unchanged while running. [Rule interactions](https://docs.optimizely.com/feature-experimentation/docs/interactions-between-flag-rules), [exclusion groups](https://docs.optimizely.com/feature-experimentation/docs/mutually-exclusive-experiments-in-feature-experimentation). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Unleash ORs strategies and ANDs constraints, and advises putting "your most restrictive activation strategy first". First match is implied, not stated (unverified). A missing context field makes a constraint false. [Activation strategies](https://docs.getunleash.io/reference/activation-strategies), [strategy variants](https://docs.getunleash.io/reference/strategy-variants). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Tang et al. (KDD 2010, Google) describe domains, layers and experiments, with at most one experiment per layer per request. Config-time checks reject an experiment that asks for traffic another has claimed. [Paper](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36500.pdf). Research (infrastructure paper, not a usability study); checked 2026-11-10.
- Braze Audience Paths use ordered first match, lock the order after launch, and end in a fixed "Everybody Else" group. Users "stay in that group even if their user profile changes". [Audience Paths](https://www.braze.com/docs/user_guide/engagement_tools/canvas/canvas_components/audience_paths/). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Customer.io Multi-split takes the first matching path, left to right, plus "All others". Its wait-until step holds a person until conditions are met or a maximum wait passes, which separates when a decision is made from which path it picks. [Multi-split](https://docs.customer.io/journeys/multi-split.md), [wait until](https://docs.customer.io/messaging/send/workflows/delays/wait-until/), [true/false branch](https://docs.customer.io/messaging/send/workflows/branches/true-false/). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- HubSpot if/then branches are checked in stacked order, with a "None met" branch, and evaluated when the record arrives. [If/then branches](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/use-if-then-branches-in-workflows). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Iterable Attribute Split: first match left to right, up to 10 branches, plus "Everyone else". [Article](https://support.iterable.com/hc/articles/16331484154132). Vendor documentation, search snippet; checked 2026-11-10. Klaviyo's conditional split ends in an "Everyone else" branch that can't be removed. [Article](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003872171-Add-a-Conditional-Split). Vendor documentation, search snippet; checked 2026-11-10.
- Qualtrics branch logic has no else, and its branches are independent rather than first match. The randomizer's "Evenly Present Elements" balances an exclusive random split. [Branch logic](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/survey-module/survey-flow/standard-elements/branch-logic/), [randomizer](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/survey-module/survey-flow/standard-elements/randomizer/). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- VAN turf cutting is exclusive by convention but not enforced, and shows an "Unassigned" legend. [Best practices](https://dflvan.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/48001155890-turf-cutting-how-to-best-practices). Common practice (third-party help page); checked 2026-11-10. Action Network ladders chain Yes/No steps down the No path. [Ladders](https://help.actionnetwork.org/hc/en-us/articles/360049606252). Vendor documentation, search snippet; checked 2026-11-10.
- **No standard found.** No formal standard or empirical study picks between ordered first-match groups and overlays, and no usability threshold caps the number of groups or rows. No journey tool documents how it handles a missing attribute. None of the flag or journey tools read warns about shadowed or empty rules. Firewall products do: [Palo Alto Panorama](https://live.paloaltonetworks.com/t5/General-Topics/Shadow-Rule-Warning/m-p/273444) and [Google Cloud Firewall Insights](https://docs.cloud.google.com/network-intelligence-center/docs/firewall-insights/concepts/insights-categories-states), the latter noting that its detection is incomplete. Both vendor documentation, search snippet; checked 2026-11-10.

### How do learning platforms model work that is required for one group and optional for another?

- In Canvas, one assignment carries "assign to" rows, and "Everyone" is relabelled "Everyone else". Overlapping rows resolve to the latest due date. Modules support "complete all" and "complete one". [UW–Superior KB](https://kb.uwsuper.edu/117955), [UTK KB](https://utk.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/2277/OIT-Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=122282), [CSU Chico KB](https://support.csuchico.edu/TDClient/1984/Portal/KB/Article/115077/Add-Requirements-to-a-Canvas-Module). Common practice (third-party university pages); checked 2026-11-10. Unverified: whether an item a student isn't assigned is dropped from module requirements.
- Moodle restricts access per activity (ALL/ANY/NOT), and course completion lists the activities that count. [Restrict access](https://docs.moodle.org/en/Restrict_access_settings), [course completion](https://docs.moodle.org/en/Course_completion_settings). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Neither LMS has a native per-group required/optional flag. This is the researcher's reading of the pages above.
- GOV.UK recommends a check-answers step before submission, and warning text for consequential information. [Check answers](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/), [warning text](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/warning-text/). Common practice (government design system); checked 2026-11-10.

### How do rule engines and standards separate evaluation errors from unknown or missing values?

- In SQL and PostgreSQL, NULL "represents 'unknown'" under Kleene K3 rules (FALSE AND NULL = FALSE, TRUE OR NULL = TRUE). Errors raise an SQLSTATE and abort the statement. WHERE keeps only TRUE rows, and CHECK passes on TRUE or NULL. [Logical operators](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-logical.html), [constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-constraints.html), [SELECT](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-select.html), [error codes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/errcodes-appendix.html). Vendor documentation of a formal standard; the ISO text is paywalled and unverified; checked 2026-11-10. K3 background: [three-valued logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-valued_logic). Common practice (reference); checked 2026-11-10.
- CEL keeps a distinct `error` value separate from unknowns, which support partial evaluation and residual expressions. Logical operators absorb errors (`error && false = false`, `error || true = true`, `error && true = error`) so that results line up with SQL and indexed queries. [Language definition](https://github.com/google/cel-spec/blob/master/doc/langdef.md), [cel-go README](https://github.com/google/cel-go/blob/master/README.md). Vendor documentation (project specification); checked 2026-11-10.
- In OPA/Rego, `undefined` propagates. Built-in runtime errors evaluate to undefined by default, which merges errors with unknowns. `strict-builtin-errors` makes them halt instead, and `default` rules supply fallbacks. [Policy language](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/policy-language). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- JSONLogic `var` takes a default, and `missing`/`missing_some` return the missing keys. No error type is documented. [Operations](https://jsonlogic.com/operations.html). Common practice (de facto spec); checked 2026-11-10.
- LaunchDarkly reports reason `ERROR` with an `errorKind` described as "abnormal". The SDK returns the fallback supplied in code. [Evaluation reasons](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/sdk/concepts/evaluation-reasons). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- OpenFeature separates reason `DEFAULT` (nothing matched) from `ERROR` with codes such as PARSE_ERROR and TARGETING_KEY_MISSING. The client "MUST NOT throw" and returns the default. [Flag evaluation](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/flag-evaluation), [types](https://openfeature.dev/specification/types). Open specification (community); checked 2026-11-10.
- Since v2.0.0, a missing attribute in the GrowthBook JS SDK fails comparisons; earlier versions treated it as 0. Error behaviour is not documented. [JS SDK](https://docs.growthbook.io/lib/js). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Statsig separates unknown-type reasons (Unrecognized, NoValues, Uninitialized, Loading) from error reasons (Error, Error:NoClient), and gates default to false. [Debugging](https://docs.statsig.com/sdk/debugging). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- FHIR R4 dataAbsentReason puts asked-unknown, not-asked, asked-declined and `temp-unknown` ("may become known") under `unknown`, with `error` a separate top-level code. [Code system](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/codesystem-data-absent-reason.html), [extension](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/extension-data-absent-reason.html). Formal standard (HL7); checked 2026-11-10.
- SpiceDB returns CONDITIONAL_PERMISSION when required context is missing and lists the missing fields, and the caller asks again. [Caveats](https://authzed.com/docs/spicedb/concepts/caveats). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Retry defaults: Temporal: 1 s initial interval, ×2 backoff, maximum interval 100× the initial, unlimited attempts, and non-retryable errors never retried. [Retry policies](https://docs.temporal.io/encyclopedia/retry-policies). Sidekiq: 25 retries over about 21 days, then a Dead set. [Error handling](https://github.com/sidekiq/sidekiq/wiki/Error-Handling). BullMQ: fixed or exponential backoff, and `UnrecoverableError` skips retries. [Retrying](https://docs.bullmq.io/guide/retrying-failing-jobs). All three are vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- OWASP A10:2025 says to "roll back the entire transaction (fail closed)", and "Fail securely" says checks "should all return false if there is an exception". [A10:2025](https://owasp.org/Top10/2025/A10_2025-Mishandling_of_Exceptional_Conditions/), [Fail securely](https://owasp.org/www-community/Fail_securely). Common practice (security guidance); checked 2026-11-10.
- SurveyJS's `empty` is true for undefined or null, and `validateExpressions()` flags unknown names at design time. [Conditional logic](https://surveyjs.io/form-library/documentation/design-survey/conditional-logic). Qualtrics "Is / Is Not Empty" tests whether a question was left blank. [Logic](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/survey-module/using-logic/). Both vendor documentation; runtime error behaviour unverified; checked 2026-11-10.

### Where do products evaluate targeting and form logic: on the client or the server?

- LaunchDarkly client SDKs receive evaluated results rather than full rulesets, so sensitive rules stay server-side, while server SDKs evaluate in-process. Secure mode HMAC-signs the context key, which authenticates identity, not attributes. [Client vs server](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/sdk/concepts/client-side-server-side), [secure mode](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/sdk/features/secure-mode). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- GrowthBook remote evaluation keeps rules on a private server and can re-evaluate when attributes change. Encrypted payloads are decrypted on the client. [Remote evaluation](https://docs.growthbook.io/self-host/remote-evaluation), [encryption](https://docs.growthbook.io/app/api#encryption). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Statsig clients fetch precomputed per-user config, with a cache fallback, or are bootstrapped by the server. [Client intro](https://docs.statsig.com/client/introduction), [Next](https://docs.statsig.com/client/Next). Unleash serves full config to backend SDKs and evaluated flags to frontends. [API](https://docs.getunleash.io/api). Both vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- SurveyJS runs async server-side validation on navigation and on completion. [Server validation](https://surveyjs.io/form-library/examples/javascript-server-side-form-validation/documentation). Zod validates the same schema in Node and in browsers. [Zod](https://zod.dev/). Both vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Relay composes component data dependencies into one request per view. React Server Function arguments are untrusted. [Relay](https://relay.dev/docs/principles-and-architecture/thinking-in-relay/), [use server](https://react.dev/reference/rsc/use-server#security-considerations). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Android offline-first guidance separates local reads from sync, classifies writes as online-only, queued or lazy, and calls for explicit versioning to resolve conflicts. [Offline-first](https://developer.android.com/topic/architecture/data-layer/offline-first). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- OWASP requires server-side validation even when the client checks the same data. [Input validation](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Input_Validation_Cheat_Sheet.html). Common practice; checked 2026-11-10. W3C's forms tutorial likewise calls for server validation alongside client validation. [Validation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/validation/). W3C guidance, not normative WCAG; checked 2026-11-10.
- RFC 9110 §13.1.1 defines strong If-Match preconditions that prevent lost updates. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-13.1.1). Formal standard; checked 2026-11-10.
- **No standard found** that establishes a combined client/server placement design. No latency measurements were collected.

### Which expression languages suit embedded admin rules, and how mature are their JS implementations?

- CEL is not Turing-complete, can use only data the host provides, and has parse, check and evaluate phases with gradual typing. [cel.dev](https://cel.dev/), [overview](https://cel.dev/overview/cel-overview), [langdef](https://github.com/cel-expr/cel-spec/blob/master/doc/langdef.md). Vendor documentation (project specification, not ISO); checked 2026-11-10.
- Kubernetes compiles and type-checks CEL when a resource is saved and estimates its cost statically. [Google OSS blog](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2023/11/kubernetes-crd-validation-using-cel.html). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- `@bufbuild/cel` (cel-es) is labelled beta. Its example shows typed environments and parse/plan/evaluate, but it was not shown to have a full checker. [cel-es](https://github.com/bufbuild/cel-es). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- `@marcbachmann/cel-js` has a checker, structural schemas and errors with source positions. Its `maxAstNodes` and `maxDepth` limits constrain structure, not runtime work. Integer literals evaluate to BigInt. It offers no editor integration. [README](https://github.com/marcbachmann/cel-js). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10. The ChromeGG `cel-js` repository is archived. [Repo](https://github.com/ChromeGG/cel-js). Checked 2026-11-10.
- jsep is a JS expression parser. The behaviour of the Alliance subset comes from the repo's own converter, evaluator and declarations. [jsep](https://ericsmekens.github.io/jsep/). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- JSONLogic stores rules as JSON trees and has its own truthiness rules (an empty array is falsy). The `json-logic-engine` compiler uses global `eval`. [Operations](https://jsonlogic.com/operations.html), [truthy](https://jsonlogic.com/truthy.html), [engine](https://github.com/json-logic/json-logic-engine). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- JSONata is Turing-complete. It has timeout, stack and sequence guards and zero/one/many sequence semantics. [Programming](https://docs.jsonata.org/programming), [guardrails](https://docs.jsonata.org/guardrails), [processing](https://docs.jsonata.org/processing). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- Other candidates (all vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10): Expr (Go) has static checking. [Expr](https://github.com/expr-lang/expr). Its TS port records skipped tests and divergences. [expr-js](https://github.com/bitcode-framework/expr-js). Jexl documents no catalog-aware checker. [Jexl](https://github.com/TomFrost/Jexl). Filtrex builds its evaluator with `new Function`. [Source](https://github.com/cshaa/filtrex/blob/master/src/filtrex.mjs). HyperFormula is GPLv3 or proprietary and works on cell values. [License](https://handsontable.com/docs/hyperformula/guide/license-key.html), [values](https://handsontable.com/docs/hyperformula/guide/types-of-values.html). Formula.js implements Excel functions only. [Formula.js](https://github.com/formulajs/formulajs). quickjs-emscripten has interrupt and memory limits. [Repo](https://github.com/justjake/quickjs-emscripten). Power Fx runs on .NET only. [Power Fx](https://github.com/microsoft/Power-Fx).
- FEEL is an OMG DMN standard expression language, implemented in JS by `feelin`. [OMG DMN](https://www.omg.org/spec/DMN/1.5/About-DMN). Formal standard; checked 2026-11-10. `feelin` describes itself as "A DMN FEEL parser and interpreter written in JavaScript", MIT-licensed, latest 8.2.0 (2026-09-20). [Repo](https://github.com/nikku/feelin), [registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/feelin). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-11.
- Expo uses Hermes as its default JS engine. [Using Hermes](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/using-hermes/). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10. ECMAScript leaves `Math.sin` and similar functions implementation-approximated, so their results can differ across engines. [ECMA-262](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/numbers-and-dates.html#sec-math.sin). Formal standard; checked 2026-11-10.
- TypeScript 6.0 is the last JS-based release, and 7.0 (Go native) shipped in July 2026 without a programmatic API. [Visual Studio Magazine](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2026/03/23/typescript-6-0-ships-as-final-javascript-based-release-clears-path-for-go-native-7-0.aspx), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/typescript-7-released/). Common practice (news); checked 2026-11-10. [typescript-go discussion 455](https://github.com/microsoft/typescript-go/discussions/455). Vendor documentation; checked 2026-11-10.
- MultiPL-E extends HumanEval and MBPP to 18 languages of varying popularity to explore how language frequency affects model performance; its abstract reports that Codex matches or exceeds its Python performance on several other languages, and states no frequency trend. [MultiPL-E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08227). Research; abstract only, checked 2026-11-11. No source here compares LLM accuracy on TypeScript with CEL. LLMs do well on simple spreadsheet formulas and worse on multi-step ones. [FLARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17330). Research. NL2Formula reaches 70.6% exact match. [NL2Formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14853). Research. Neither formula study compares against JS. FLARE and NL2Formula checked 2026-11-10.

### What does research say about non-experts authoring Boolean rules?

- Pane & Myers (2000) found AND and NOT read differently in different contexts and parentheses hard to use. Untrained users wrote better queries in a tabular notation than in textual Boolean. [Paper](https://john.pane.net/pdf/PaneMyersTabularTextual.pdf). Research; checked 2026-11-10.
- Tanin et al. (2000, within-subjects, n=12) found that previews of distribution and result size gave faster performance and higher satisfaction. [Paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Tanin2000Facilitating.pdf). Research; checked 2026-11-10.
- Panko (EuSpRIG 2000) synthesises studies showing spreadsheet errors are common and consequential. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3457). Research (synthesis); checked 2026-11-10.
- Baymard checkout testing observed frustration when validation fired before input was complete. [Inline validation](https://baymard.com/research-articles/inline-form-validation). Research (usability testing); checked 2026-11-10.
- NN/g: most users struggle with advanced or Boolean search (website search, not rule authoring), and most never customise defaults. [Search](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/search-visible-and-simple/), [defaults](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/the-power-of-defaults/). Common practice (research-informed); checked 2026-11-10.

### "Previous answer": four meanings in the code (`81af37d5a0`)

1. **Cohort `FormFieldValue`** matches *any* live response ever submitted, using `String(answer)` equality or truthiness. Withdrawals are included. In the "any answer" mode `false`, 0, `""` and an empty list count as absent and whitespace-only text counts as an answer; the equals mode compares `String(answer)`, so 0 matches `"0"`. `server/src/actions/cohort-resolver.service.ts:215`, `cohort-expression.evaluator.ts:204`.
2. **Visibility `sourceFormId`** reads the *latest* response by `createdAt DESC`, with no id tie-break and no withdrawal filter. A question missing from that response does not fall back to an older answer. Its blank test, `hasContent`, counts `0` and `false` as answers and an empty list and whitespace-only text as blank, unlike the cohort "any answer" mode in item 1. `server/src/tasks/member-visibility.service.ts:117`, `common/src/forms/visibility.ts:84`.
3. **Variables `sourceField`/`sourceList`** read the *full history* (`createdAt ASC, id ASC`), withdrawals included. `form-response-query.service.ts:116`, `common/src/forms/variable-evaluation.ts:159`.
4. **Aggregates** take each member's latest response that is not a withdrawal, dedupe options and exclude guests. Because filtering happens before selection, an older response can surface. `server/src/tasks/variable-aggregates.ts:25`.

Storage: responses have no completed/amended/voided lifecycle and no revision lineage (`formresponse.entity.ts:44`). Every submission writes a new row (`form-response-writer.service.ts:81`). Hidden answers are stripped before saving (`form-submission-validation.service.ts:167`), so a missing key can mean hidden, unanswered, or absent from that form version.

### Required and optional work, and the missed-suite streak (code read; demo script)

- The streak producer drops optional, onboarding and suiteless actions (`action-suspension.service.ts:49,120,182`). A suite closes at its latest required deadline (`missed-suite-streak.ts:2,28`).
- Per suite: With no required assignment, the streak is unchanged. With all required work completed or withdrawn, the streak resets. With any required action missed, one miss is added.
- The threshold is 3 consecutive misses within the signing period. Completing optional work neither offsets a miss nor resets the streak.
- Demo: `miss → no required → miss` gives 2 misses, and `miss → miss → no required → miss` gives 3.
- Suites 16 and 32 have launch reminders with `excludeOptionalActions=false`. This was a settings check only; delivery was not queried.
