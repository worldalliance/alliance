# Evidence

## Use of colour on the action-history bars

Question: do a colour key and screen-reader names let the action-history bars, which tell statuses apart by colour, meet WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1, Use of Color? Checked 2026-11-24.

- [WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.1 Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color) (W3C Recommendation, 12 December 2024; formal standard), Level A: "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element."
- [Understanding SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) (W3C, informative, "not required to meet WCAG"): "This criterion requires a visible alternative to color", and it advises using "information in addition to color, such as shape or text". It doesn't say whether a key alone is enough; its one key example pairs the key with numbers on the image, and its sufficient techniques add a pattern (G111) or text (G14).

Finding: no. A key explains the colours and the names reach screen readers, but colour stays the only visual means of telling one bar from another, so the criterion wants a second visual cue such as a pattern or shape.
