## Decisions

- **Policy.** "May manage group X's invites and invite requests" is `canManageCommunity(user, X)`: an admin, or a leader of X. The guard on every such endpoint, `CommunityLeaderGuard`, has admitted admins since commit `5ef63153f` ("allow admins in community leader guard"), and admins could already create, edit the group of, and delete invites for any group, so the leader-only service checks were the outliers.
- **One refusal.** `assertCanManageCommunity`, beside `canManageCommunity`, throws 400 `User is not a leader of community <id>` for anyone the rule refuses, so each site checking the rule shares its refusal too. Sites that refused with a different message before (`updateCommunity`, `createCommunityInvite`, both invite deletes) keep theirs; unifying those messages is not needed to unify the rule.
- **Answering a community invite request** (`POST /community/communityInvites/:inviteId/{approve,reject}Request`) and **a one-time invite request** (`POST /user/onetimeInvite/:inviteId/{approve,reject}`) move to `canManageCommunity`. Their leader-only checks date from the features' first versions (#20, #24) with no recorded reason, and their own guard deliberately admits admins; #443 left them alone only as out of its scope. Approving a request yields an invite an admin could already create directly, so admitting admins grants nothing new. `findPendingInviteRequestForLeader` is renamed `findPendingInviteRequestForManager` to match. A one-time invite request with no group (its group was deleted) is still refused, now with 400 `Invite request has no group` instead of the misleading `User is not a leader of community null`: the answer notifies through the group, so there is nothing to answer.
- **`removeUserFromCommunity` and `deleteCommunity` stay leader-only.** Admins have separate endpoints (`POST /community/:id/removeMember/admin`, `DELETE /community/:id/admin`) whose behavior differs on purpose: the admin removal notifies as "Alliance staff" and may remove a leader, and the admin delete skips the leader path's requirement that the group be otherwise empty.
- **Listing a group's invites follows the same policy.** `GET /community/communityInvites/community/:communityId` found the group by "led by this user", so an admin got an error while being able to answer those same requests; it now asks `canManageCommunity`, and a non-manager gets 400 `User is not a leader of community <id>` instead of the entity-not-found error. `GET /user/onetimeInvites/:communityId` checked nothing past `CommunityLeaderGuard`, so a leader of any group could list another group's one-time invites, codes included; it now asks `canManageCommunity` too. Both are copies of the rule, found while searching for its implementations; the second is an authorization gap closed by the same check. Only the group's own invites tab calls either endpoint.
- **Editing and deleting a one-time invite share one rule**, `canModifyOnetimeInvite` beside the entity: the inviter, or anyone who may manage the invite's group (`canManageCommunity`). Delete already worked this way, and #443 added an end-to-end test for a leader deleting a member's invite, so that permission is deliberate; edit (inviter or admin, from #135 with no recorded reason) widens to match rather than delete narrowing. A leader who may delete and recreate a member's invite gains nothing by also being able to edit it. Retargeting an edited invite still requires managing the new group. Edit keeps its 400 `Invite belongs to someone else` and delete its bare 400.
- **Share-link group targeting (`assertLeadsCommunity`) stays leader-only, admins included.** It is not "manage group X's invites": it aims the caller's own reusable link, and signup placement (`selectCommunityForReferral` in `contract.service.ts`) places a member into a link's named group only if the link's owner still leads it, treating that as the leader's own consent that bypasses `maxCapacity`. An admin's link aimed at a group they don't lead would place nobody. A comment on the helper and an end-to-end test of the admin refusal pin this.
