## Question

What established standards and widely adopted practices exist for UI quality, UX and reducing friction, and how do they apply to the Alliance agent desktop app and the main Alliance product (web, mobile, admin)? Researched by a Codex agent with live web search on 5 November 2026; its report follows as sections 1 to 6, edited only to mark where the repository has changed since and to correct a finding later checked against its source; section 7 records sources checked after it. All its links were checked to resolve on 5 November 2026, except iso.org's three pages, which refuse automated fetches and are unverified.

## 1. Landscape

Labels used below:

- **F — Formal standard:** normative requirements within its stated scope.
- **V — Platform vendor recommendation:** authoritative for that platform, not universally binding.
- **R — Research-backed practice:** evidence supports it, subject to the study’s context.
- **C — Common practice:** established implementation pattern, not proof of usability.

| Source                                     | Status | Authority and useful scope                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **WCAG 2.2**                               | **F**  | The strongest source of testable accessibility requirements. Covers web content, including Electron’s web interface. It does not establish overall usability or visual quality. Latest Recommendation remains **12 December 2024**. [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **ISO 9241-110:2020; -210:2019; -11:2018** | **F**  | Respectively: interaction principles, human-centred design processes, and usability definitions. Useful for evaluating task suitability and design process; not component specifications. ISO confirms -110 and -210 remained current after their **2025 reviews**; -11 was confirmed in 2023. Public abstracts checked; full paid texts were not reviewed. [110](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html), [210](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html), [11](https://www.iso.org/standard/63500.html) |
| **Apple HIG**                              | **V**  | Primary reference for macOS/iOS controls, keyboard conventions, accessibility settings, notifications and navigation. Accessibility guidance was substantially revised in **March 2025**; Liquid Glass guidance arrived June 2025 and changed September 2025. Visual fashion should not override legibility. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)           |
| **Material Design 3**                      | **V**  | Relevant to Android interaction and accessibility. **M3 Expressive**, introduced in May 2025, changes components and motion guidance; it is Google’s design direction, not universal consensus on appearance. [Google’s research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research), [motion](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion)                                                                                                                                               |
| **GNOME HIG**                              | **V**  | Useful Linux guidance for keyboard access, progress, dialogs and notifications. Its conventions are specific to GNOME, not every Linux desktop. [Guidelines](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines.html)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **GOV.UK Design System; USWDS**            | **C**  | Strong, maintained institutional patterns for forms, errors, task lists, tables and plain communication. Adopt the relevant patterns; their entire visual language and service workflows need not transfer. [GOV.UK patterns](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/), [USWDS principles](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-principles/)                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Nielsen Norman Group; Baymard**          | **R**  | NN/g offers broad usability heuristics; Baymard provides detailed empirical findings, principally for ecommerce. Baymard’s form-effort findings are useful hypotheses for Alliance onboarding, not evidence that its checkout conversion numbers transfer. [NN/g heuristics, reviewed 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [Baymard form research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)                                                 |
| **Established products**                   | **C**  | Evidence of familiar patterns, not standards or independent validation. Their documented behaviours inform the desktop recommendations below.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Core Web Vitals**                        | **V**  | Google’s widely adopted web-performance thresholds, grounded in research. They measure loading, responsiveness and layout stability—not complete UX. [Threshold methodology, updated May 2025](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

Two standards distinctions matter:

- **WCAG2ICT is informative guidance**, not a separate conformance standard. Its current **11 December 2025** Note explains applying WCAG to native software, useful for React Native and desktop integration. [WCAG2ICT](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict/)
- WCAG 2.2 became **ISO/IEC 40500:2025**, matching the October 2023 text. W3C still describes the December 2024 text’s ISO adoption as forthcoming. **WCAG 3 remains a draft**, including its September 2026 update. [WCAG status](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/), [WCAG 3 status](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/wcag3-intro/)

**Consensus:** accessible operation, visible status, understandable labels, predictable controls and recoverable errors recur across these sources. Exact layouts, density, animation, onboarding length and agent interaction mechanics remain contextual. [WCAG](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-principles/)

## 2. Criteria

These are proposed Alliance acceptance criteria. **F** items reflect standards; **V/R/C** items operationalize guidance and may need a documented exception. This is a focused checklist, not the entirety of WCAG.

Record **pass, fail, not applicable, or untested**, with evidence. A screenshot can establish a visual defect; it cannot establish keyboard or screen-reader correctness.

**Accessibility and input**

- [ ] **F:** Every ordinary action works using a keyboard, including sidebar selection, disclosure controls, attachments and interruption. No interaction traps keyboard focus. [WCAG keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [no keyboard trap](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap)
- [ ] **F:** Keyboard focus is visible and is never entirely hidden behind sticky headers, composers or overlays. [Focus visible](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible), [focus not obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)
- [ ] **F:** Controls expose correct accessible names, roles and states; screen readers can distinguish selected navigation, expanded cards, disabled actions and checked options. [Name, role, value](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html)
- [ ] **F:** Text contrast is at least **4.5:1**, or **3:1 for large text**—18 pt regular or 14 pt bold. Required control/state indicators meet **3:1** against adjacent colours; status does not depend on colour alone. [Text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), [colour](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color)
- [ ] **F:** Web text remains usable at **200% enlargement**; ordinary content reflows at **320 CSS px** without two-dimensional scrolling. Necessary tables/diagrams may retain two-dimensional layouts. [Resize](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html), [reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)
- [ ] **F:** Web pointer targets are at least **24 × 24 CSS px**, or satisfy a documented WCAG spacing/equivalent-control/other exception. Measure the hit area, not just the icon. [Target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- [ ] **V:** Use **44 × 44 pt iOS** and **48 × 48 dp Android** hit areas as Alliance’s default touch targets. Apple currently distinguishes **44 pt default / 28 pt minimum** on iOS and **28 pt default / 20 pt minimum** on macOS; these are not interchangeable with WCAG’s CSS-pixel rule. [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Android](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)
- [ ] **F:** Dragging has a non-drag pointer alternative: file picker for attachments, move controls for reorderable content. Keyboard access is additionally required. [Dragging movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- [ ] **C:** Opening a modal moves focus inside it; Tab stays inside; closing restores a sensible focus location. Escape closes ordinary dismissible dialogs. [WAI dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/)
- [ ] **F:** Meaningful images have text alternatives; prerecorded synchronized media with audio has captions, unless it is itself a media alternative for text, and necessary alternatives for visual information. [Text alternatives](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#text-alternatives), [media](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#time-based-media), [captions (prerecorded)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html)

**Feedback and system status**

- [ ] **R:** After starting an operation, the interface visibly distinguishes working, completed and failed states. A failure cannot appear as an empty successful result. [Visibility and error heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)
- [ ] **V:** Long-running work names the operation; determinate progress appears only when progress is measurable. Unknown-duration work uses indeterminate status rather than an invented percentage. [GNOME progress](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/patterns/feedback/progress-bars.html)
- [ ] **F:** Save, upload, validation and completion status messages are available to assistive technology without moving focus to them. [Status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)

**Forms, error prevention and recovery**

- [ ] **F:** Every input has an identifiable label; required formats and constraints are explained before submission. [Labels and instructions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/labels-or-instructions.html)
- [ ] **F:** Authentication supports password managers and paste, or another compliant assistance/alternative mechanism; it does not require unaided password recall or code transcription. [Accessible authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html)
- [ ] **F:** Information already entered in the same process is prefilled or selectable unless a stated security, validity or essential-use exception applies. [Redundant entry](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/redundant-entry.html)
- [ ] **C:** Invalid submission identifies each affected field and how to correct it; multi-field forms provide an error summary linked to fields. Previously entered answers remain available. [GOV.UK error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/), [validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)
- [ ] **R:** Optional or irrelevant questions do not block the task. Every required field has a task-related reason; conditional fields appear only when relevant. This adapts ecommerce evidence rather than asserting an Alliance-specific research finding. [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)

**Onboarding and empty states**

- [ ] **C:** A multi-step flow identifies the current step or outstanding tasks; users can revisit completed steps without re-entering answers. [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/), [task list](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/task-list/)
- [ ] **V:** Empty states explain why content is absent and offer the relevant next action, such as creating the first task or clearing a search. [GNOME placeholders](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/patterns/feedback/placeholders.html)
- [ ] **R:** Advanced settings can stay collapsed, but information required to finish the current task remains visible. [Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)

**Navigation, consistency and copy**

- [ ] **R:** The current location is identifiable; equivalent actions retain the same names and behaviour across screens. [Consistency and recognition heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)
- [ ] **V:** Standard editing/navigation shortcuts retain platform meanings; shortcuts are discoverable through menus or help. Use macOS conventions on Mac and the appropriate Linux conventions on Linux. [Apple keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/keyboards), [GNOME shortcuts](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/reference/keyboard.html)
- [ ] **R:** Unfamiliar or ambiguous icons have visible text labels. An accessible name or hover tooltip alone does not establish that a sighted novice understands the action. [Icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)
- [ ] **R:** User-facing errors name the problem and a recovery action in familiar language; raw codes or technical output are supplementary detail. [Error recovery heuristic](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)

**Performance, notifications and motion**

- [ ] **R:** Local interaction feedback targets roughly **100 ms**; a continuing wait is visibly acknowledged by roughly **1 second**; extended work permits attention to move elsewhere. These are operational targets based on longstanding guidance, not universal deadlines. [Response-time guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)
- [ ] **V:** Web performance meets the Core Web Vitals thresholds in section 5; loading content does not unexpectedly displace the control being used. [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)
- [ ] **V:** Notifications are not duplicated for the same event, avoid confidential previews, and open the relevant content. Essential outcomes remain discoverable inside the app when notifications are disabled. [Apple notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)
- [ ] **V:** With Reduce Motion enabled, nonessential zooming, scaling and repetitive animation are removed or reduced while state changes remain understandable. [Apple accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

**Trust and consequential actions**

- [ ] **F:** Submissions involving legal commitments, financial transactions or covered stored-data changes provide a qualifying reversal, error-checking/correction, or review/confirmation mechanism. WCAG does **not** require a confirmation dialog for every action. [Error prevention](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html)
- [ ] **V:** Destructive UI actions identify what will be affected and provide confirmation or undo; their buttons say what they do. [GNOME dialogs](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/patterns/feedback/dialogs.html)

## 3. Applying them to the desktop app

The [desktop requirements](../01a1a949-c8f1-7000-9631-5610045dff02-alliance-agent-desktop-app/REQUIREMENTS.md) establish non-technical employees, no terminal UI, no agent permission prompts, no telemetry, local execution that stops on quit, and specific transcript-sharing rules. Those constraints take precedence over competitor imitation.

## 5. Measuring friction

| Method                          | Measurement to use                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Can agents apply it without users?                                                                |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Task success — R**            | Define the correct end state first. Report successful attempts, assisted completions, failures and abandonment separately. [Task-success measurement](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/success-rate-the-simplest-usability-metric/)                                                                 | **Partly:** scripted completion establishes functional success, not human discoverability.        |
| **Time on task and errors — R** | Record completion time, errors, retries and recovery time for the same tasks; distinguish user effort from system waiting. Report distributions and sample size, not just an average. [Quantitative usability methods](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/quantitative-user-research-methods/)        | **Partly:** agents measure system waits and repeatable paths; agent speed is not human task time. |
| **SUS — R**                     | Ten-item perceived-usability questionnaire, scored 0–100. The score is **not a percentage**; 68 is a historical benchmark, not a universal pass mark. [SUS interpretation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/measuring-perceived-usability/)                                                         | **No:** agents can administer/score it, but must not invent participant responses.                |
| **UMUX-Lite — R**               | Two items covering perceived usefulness and ease, conventionally seven-point responses. Keep wording/scoring consistent; do not silently substitute the newer UX-Lite instrument. [Researcher summary](https://measuringu.com/what-ux-metrics-has-measuringu-created/)                              | **No:** requires people’s perceptions.                                                            |
| **HEART — R**                   | Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success; choose goals, then signals, then metrics. It is a framework, not one composite score. [Original Google research](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/) | Agents can define metrics; actual happiness and usage require people/data.                        |
| **Cognitive walkthrough — R**   | At every step assess whether a novice would form the right goal, notice the action, understand its effect and recognize progress. [Method](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cognitive-walkthroughs/)                                                                                                | **Yes, as inspection:** conclusions remain hypotheses about users.                                |
| **Heuristic evaluation — R**    | Inspect realistic tasks against the checklist; report the failed criterion, evidence, consequence and severity. [Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)                                                                                                            | **Yes:** useful for finding likely problems, not validating satisfaction.                         |
| **Steps/clicks/fields — R**     | Count required decisions, entries, repeated information and app switches alongside clicks. There is no valid universal three-click limit. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-click-rule/), [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)                | **Yes:** useful comparative diagnostics, not standalone UX scores.                                |

**Core Web Vitals — V:** at the **75th percentile**, evaluated separately for mobile and desktop:

| Metric                           |    Good | Needs improvement |    Poor |
| -------------------------------- | ------: | ----------------: | ------: |
| LCP — loading                    |  ≤2.5 s |          >2.5–4 s |    >4 s |
| INP — interaction responsiveness | ≤200 ms |       >200–500 ms | >500 ms |
| CLS — layout instability         |    ≤0.1 |         >0.1–0.25 |   >0.25 |

All three must be good to meet the overall criterion. **INP replaced FID on 12 March 2024.** These thresholds concern web experiences, not native-app startup or agent completion time. [Thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds), [INP change](https://web.dev/blog/inp-cwv-march-12)

Agents can run browser walkthroughs, keyboard checks, accessibility scans and controlled performance measurements. A standard Lighthouse navigation run cannot measure INP without interaction; laboratory results do not establish real-user percentile performance. Automated accessibility checks also leave manual checks outstanding. [Playwright accessibility testing](https://playwright.dev/docs/accessibility-testing), [lab/field distinction](https://web.dev/articles/vitals), [W3C testing limits](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/)

My suggested repeatable task set:

- **Desktop:** first task; failed setup recovery; attach screenshot; interrupt; switch/resume sessions; open preview; inspect failed CI; open read-only transcript.
- **Member:** invitation → agreement → first action; failed submission recovery; group invitation; reminder → action; membership reactivation.
- **Admin:** find a member; edit an action; correct invalid form; preview; schedule/publish to a specified audience.

For each, test the normal path plus slow network, failure and interruption. Use Playwright for web/Electron-facing behaviour and native-device testing for React Native behaviour.

For the desktop app, respect the explicit **no-telemetry requirement**: use controlled local benchmarks and separately arranged employee usability sessions. For the main product, I would prioritize **successful completion with low effort**, not time spent in the app. Both are product-specific measurement choices.

## 6. Gaps requiring judgment

- **No universal aesthetic standard.** WCAG and ISO do not settle spacing scales, visual density, typography personality or whether glass/material effects are desirable. ISO 9241-110 explicitly excludes aesthetics and corporate identity. [ISO scope](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html)
- **No settled agent-chat interaction standard emerged from these sources.** Tool-card density, auto-scroll, queue versus steer, and Stop versus Pause vary. Cursor’s configurable density and distinct queue semantics illustrate the choices. [Density](https://cursor.com/changelog/3-4), [message handling](https://cursor.com/docs/agent/overview)
- **No universal optimum onboarding length.** Splitting a difficult decision across screens can help even when it adds clicks. Required reading, account creation timing and optional tours need task-specific evaluation. [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/), [three-click evidence](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-click-rule/)
- **Less friction is not always the right outcome.** Deliberate review is appropriate for consequential commitments or destructive changes. For this desktop app, preserve the specified prompt-free agent execution; assess truthful status, interruption and reviewable outcomes without silently changing that policy. [WCAG error prevention](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html)
- **Agent inspection cannot establish human ease.** It can expose defects and produce testable hypotheses. Understanding unfamiliar icons, confidence in an agent’s work and satisfaction still require representative employees and members—including people using assistive technology. [Cognitive walkthrough limitations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cognitive-walkthroughs/), [W3C evaluation limits](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/)
