## Actions page

- "Draft or active" uses the existing status buckets (`actionStatusBucket`): Draft and Active. Onboarding and archived actions don't count. Each suite links to its suite page and shows its active and draft counts.

## Where the flow lives

- "Begin launch process" shows only while the suite has no members-taking-action event, so a launched suite never offers a second launch. The launch itself reloads the suite right before creating anything and refuses when one exists, so a launch from another tab or person since the page loaded creates no second announcement and reminder set. Two launches confirmed in the same second can't both go through either: the server allows one live members-taking-action event per action (`UQ_action_event_one_member_action`), so the second fails before creating anything.
- A suite with an office taking action event but no members one shows, where the button was, why it can't launch (`existingLaunchEvent`), since the launch page would refuse it anyway. A launched suite shows nothing there.
- Everything runs on the client against existing endpoints: `evaluate-cohort`, the admin action and task-form queries, the suite event endpoint, and reminder group creation. No server change was needed.

## Schedule

- "PST" is read as Pacific time (`America/Los_Angeles`), so the defaults follow daylight saving. Both dates stay editable, with the Pacific time shown under each picker.
- Every Pacific time label on the launch page comes from `LAUNCH_TIME_ZONE` and `inLaunchZone` in `apps/admin/src/lib/launchTimeZone.ts`, the helpers the Away members page uses, not from a copy in the suite launch code. `inLaunchZone` formats with a fixed pattern (`Oct 28, 2026 at 7:00 PM PT`), so the labels read the same on every platform; the launch-zone-stable-format task records why. Agent decision.
- The office event is 11:59pm Pacific, seven days after the day members start. When the member start rolls past midnight, the office event moves with it.
- The office event uses the `office_action` status ("Office taking action"), matching `CreateEventForm`'s deadline default.
- A suite that already has an office taking action event can't launch (`existingLaunchEvent`). Nor can one with an event of any status dated from the chosen members' start to the chosen office deadline (`eventBeforeDeadline`, a blocker under Schedule): members' deadline is the first event after they start, whatever its status, so such an event would end members' time before the new office event. An event before members start or after the office deadline doesn't change their deadline and stays. Agent decision from review; origin otherwise unrecorded.
- How long members get reads as whole days counted the way the contract check counts them, by Pacific wall-clock time, then the real hours and minutes past the last whole day, so it never contradicts the contract banner. On the day clocks fall back, that remainder can read 24 hours or more.
- While the schedule is valid, the Schedule check states "Members have <duration> to complete their actions" in black body text under its title, not in the grey summary beside it. The origin of this placement and color is unrecorded.

## Sanity checks

- A member's actions are the suite actions whose cohort expression includes them, evaluated with `evaluate-cohort`, which counts active users only. For a regular (non-onboarding) action, only members with an active contract count, since the server admits a member only while they hold one; contract status is read as of now, not as of the launch time. For an onboarding action, only members who have never signed count, since the server admits one only if they never signed or first signed at or after its start, which at launch is still to come. A member away at any point between members and office taking action doesn't count either, since the server leaves them out of the roster and its reminders; away dates are read for the year ahead, or through office taking action when that is later. Prerequisites are ignored, so the totals are an upper bound: someone who would only unlock a later action still counts it. Optional actions count too.
- "Preview members' actions" carries the requirement to flag members whose actions add up to more than 15 minutes: its summary says how many members get more than 15 minutes, and the minutes of each action combination over 15 show red beside a warning icon, with screen-reader text saying it is over 15 minutes, and a button that previews one of its members. While any member count is unknown, the summary says the minutes are unknown and the check doesn't pass. Members aren't listed by name. No user requirement records folding the check into the table or dropping the names; its origin is unrecorded, so a later agent may reconsider it.
- Contract violations show in a red banner directly above the launch button (a placement whose origin is unrecorded), below only a failed launch's error, which stays next to the button that was clicked, and block launch until staff tick "Launch anyway, breaking the contract". Two contract terms are checkable at launch: a member assigned more than 15 minutes (the contract's 15-minute blocks), and office taking action less than 7 days after members start (its 7-day completion period), counted by Pacific wall-clock time, so a deadline seven days out falls short when it is earlier in the day than members start, and the default 11:59pm deadline holds across a daylight saving change. Its non-overlap term needs other suites' periods per member, left out with the cross-suite load. Its exceptions depend on a member's circumstances, which no launch check can see.
- Readiness reuses the action dashboard's checklist, now extracted to `actionReadiness.ts`, so both screens apply the same rules, including the dashboard's "Image set" and "Task form has an output view, for the completion card" items: an action missing a cover image or a task form with an output view blocks launch until staff fix it or dismiss the item. It reads each action through the admin action query, because suite payloads don't load authors or the suite relation.
- Member preview lists each picked member's assigned actions, their total minutes, preview and edit links, and a "Log in as this member" impersonation link (the same endpoint the user detail page uses). Impersonating replaces this browser's auth cookies with the member's and nothing switches back, so the preview says in words that it signs the browser out of admin, and that launch progress survives signing back in.
- The launch flow is its own page, `/suites/:suiteId/launch`. The schedule, "Ready to launch" boxes, dismissals, and the contract acknowledgment are saved in this browser's localStorage per suite, so a refresh or leaving and coming back keeps them; a successful launch clears them. They are per browser, not shared between staff.
- Saved progress can't vouch for what nobody reviewed. A "Ready to launch" box is saved with the version it approved: the action's fields, its forms' `formSnapshotId`s, and each A/B variant's name and share. It clears with an "Edited since it was marked ready" note once any of them changes. The version leaves out only what changes without a staff edit (timestamps, participant counts, the computed autocomplete time, the custom stat's value, priority, events, status, updates, and the member-action dates), what depends on who is viewing, relations the readiness checklist re-checks on every load (suite, project, authors) or no launch check reads (reviewers, and each follow-up form's nested form), and the stored image columns `storedImage` and `storedSquareThumbnailImage`, which `image` and `squareThumbnailImage` repeat as urls, so a field added later clears the box by default. While any check fails to load, a box stays as saved and no row reads edited, since a failed load leaves parts out of the version; the load error blocks launch meanwhile. The server bumps `updatedAt` and the participant counts on every action every 10 minutes. When the browser can't write saved progress, the page says it isn't saving progress, so a reload starting over doesn't come as a surprise; when saved progress can't be read but can be written, it says the saved progress couldn't be read. The contract acknowledgment is saved with the exact violations it accepted, so a new or different violation asks again. The schedule is saved only once staff edit it, so a later visit pre-fills the default afresh rather than an old default that may now be in the past or under 30 minutes away. An edited schedule whose member start has passed falls back to the default schedule.
- n in "I am going to notify {n} real members" is the size of the union of the suite's action cohorts: the members assigned at least one of its actions. The group leads the Group Leads reminders reach aren't counted unless they're assigned an action themselves: who they are depends on who hasn't finished when each reminder sends, so no count at launch is exact, and the required copy says "members". A reviewer, Charles Lien, left this choice to the agent. The launch confirmation's message adds a sentence saying so whenever a reminder goes to group leads, without changing the copy staff type.
- The launch confirmation restates the member start and office deadline in Pacific time, the window between them, and how many reminders it creates, so an AM/PM or wrong-week pick shows at the last step, where the pickers show local time.
- The typed confirmation is required in every environment, unlike the reminders tab's, which skips it in development.

- Each blocker above the launch button names the section that resolves it (`LaunchSection`, whose values are the sections' DOM ids), and clicking it scrolls there. Blockers with nowhere to go (already launched, still loading, a check that failed to load) stay plain text.

## Spelling and grammar

- Rule checks: double spaces between words (a markdown hard break, two trailing spaces, is allowed), back-to-back repeated words, a space before punctuation, and a missing space after sentence punctuation.
- Spelling counts a word as known when either `typo-js`'s bundled en_US Hunspell dictionary (which handles inflections) or `an-array-of-english-words`, a 275k-word list with newer and compound words, has it. On the 114 local actions this cut misspelling flags from 67 (29 distinct words) to 29 (13), and the long list has some rare or archaic words that are mostly typos in practice ("wich", "wether", "persue", "calender", "publically"), so for those only Hunspell decides. A larger Hunspell dictionary (`dictionary-en`) didn't help: it is the same word list. Treating a word that splits into two known words as known let real typos through ("arguement"). Both dictionaries load lazily, only when the launch panel opens (about 190 KB and 730 KB gzipped). Only lowercase words of three or more letters are checked, so names and acronyms aren't flagged. Curly apostrophes count as straight ones, so `isn’t` is checked whole. URLs (including bare `www.` ones and bare lowercase domains, so a missing space before "Net" or "Us" is still caught), link targets, inline code, code fence labels, file names, HTML tags, and email addresses are skipped.
- Capitalized words stay unchecked even at the start of a sentence, line, or heading. Checking them there (known if either case is in a dictionary) took the local actions from 67 misspelling flags to 266, nearly all brand names opening form option labels, and found no new typo.
- A short list of Alliance terms (`grantmaking`, `grantmaker`, `grantmakers`) always counts as spelled right. A rule accepting any known word plus "making" was the alternative, but compound rules let real typos through.
- Dismissing a misspelling dismisses that word everywhere in the suite, possessives included, like "ignore word" in a spellchecker; other findings are dismissed one at a time. So findings are listed by what one dismissal covers: a misspelling is one row naming how many places it's in, each place with its context and edit link, and the launch blocker counts those rows. "Show dismissed" lists each dismissal once, greyed, with a button to restore it, since a dismissal is saved until launch and one stray click could otherwise hide a real typo for good.
- Checked text: the action's name, short description, body, and every member-facing text in its task form and in each variant's form, since members split across variants each see one. A form finding links to the form builder with the page or element its text came from selected, or its page when the element's id is missing or the builder resolves it elsewhere (ids may repeat, across list sub-fields and accordion parts too), or the form at its first page when the page's id doesn't reach the page either, so a link never selects an element that doesn't hold the text. Both sides resolve an id through one function, `placeForSelect`, so they can't drift. The builder's select-on-load link therefore finds any page or element, not only questions; a dependency link to another form's question that is gone selects a page or element with the same id if there is one, without the "no page or element" error. That takes a hand-written id, since the builder gives every page and element a random UUID and the JSON editor refuses a new id that collides, so the link doesn't carry a separate question-only kind. The origin of this selection is unrecorded. A variant form's saved version is part of the action's "Ready to launch" version too, so editing a variant clears the box.
- Fixing means editing the action (an edit link opens it in a new tab) and re-running the checks. There is no in-place auto-fix. A dismissal is keyed by the finding's surrounding text, so it survives edits elsewhere in the same field. Findings with identical surrounding text, as when a form repeats a sentence, are numbered in order so each is dismissed on its own.
- A finding in an action's name, short description, or body links to the action's details tab with that field focused and the flagged text selected, scrolled to the middle of the admin layout's content pane (`textarea-caret` finds the selection's line). The body grows to fit its text, so only the pane scrolls; the name and short description are fixed-height and scroll inside themselves, so each first scrolls the selection's line to its own middle. The link carries the field, the range, and the flagged text; when the field is missing, the range unreadable, or the text at the range is no longer the flagged text (the field was edited since the check), it selects nothing and says why in the dashboard's error banner, naming the field by its label on the action page. The origin of this behavior is unrecorded.

## Launch

- The sequence is: create the member event, then the office event, then the default reminder groups on the new member event. The default reminders come from the same list the reminders tab's "Populate default reminders" uses, now `defaultReminderBodies` in `presets.ts`.
- The steps aren't atomic. When one fails, the panel stays open and says exactly what was created and what's left to add by hand.

## Launch review additions

- Distribution: members are grouped by the exact set of suite actions they get, most common first, with each group's minutes. A button per group adds one of its members to the preview, so each distinct experience is one click from "Log in as this member".
- Reminder preview: the default reminders are listed by send time in Pacific time, computed with the office action event as the deadline. A reminder that would send before members start blocks launch, which happens when the window is shorter than three days.
- Each reminder opens to its email subject, email, text, and push as a member receives them at its send time, rendered by `renderReminderKeywords`, which moved from the server's `processKeywordReplacements` into `common/src/reminderKeywords.ts` with `formatActionList` and `getTimeLeftString`, so the launch page renders exactly what the worker sends. The server keeps `processKeywordReplacements` as a wrapper that supplies its site URL, from which the renderer builds the `#{link}` and `#{grouplink}` URLs for both. "Preview as" picks a group of members by the actions they get, most common first, and renders for that group's first member, since the actions change the task names and counts. It shows the groups as a grid of cards, each with its member count, task count, minutes, and action names, built from radio inputs so arrow keys move between them, the picked one marked by a check icon as well as its green border; no user requirement records the cards, so their origin is unrecorded and a later agent may reconsider them. Group leads reminders render for a sample group with 3 members still to finish. A member's uncompleted tasks at launch are all their assigned suite actions, less optional ones where the reminder excludes them. The email mockup shows its body as the HTML the server sends, built by the same `reminderEmailHtml` in `common/src/reminderKeywords.ts` (each newline a line break), with the email template's rule scaling images to fit, in a sandboxed frame, since keyword values such as a member's name are member-entered text.
- The missed deadline message's preview shows each copy a member can get, since nothing at launch knows which applies to each recipient. The server sends the group's own copy only on a member's first miss in the MissedSuiteFirstNotice experiment's control arm, and there `#{missedactioncontext}` reads differently on a member's first assigned suite (a longer paragraph about the contract) than on later ones; the variant arm's first miss and every second miss in a row send fixed copy, which lives in `common/src/missedSuiteNotice.ts` so the server and the preview read one copy, and one rule for which groups those are (`isMissedSuiteReminderGroup`: `#{missedactioncontext}` or `#{secondmisswarning}` in the email subject or body). The four previews are labelled "First miss (control arm), on a member's first assigned suite", "First miss (control arm), for a member assigned suites before", "First miss (variant arm)" and "Second miss in a row". A third miss in a row sends the suspension notice instead, which this reminder doesn't preview. A reviewer, Charles Lien, left the first-suite choice to the agent; the arm and second-miss previews are an agent decision under the requirement that the launch show each reminder's actual text; Mark's intent is unrecorded.
- A reminder sent at one instant for everyone (the launch announcement and the `from_deadline` reminders) notes how many recipients it reaches between 10pm and 8am in their saved zone, Pacific when unset. Range reminders already send at each member's preferred time, so they're never noted. The note doesn't block. The default schedule trips it: "Missed deadline message" sends at 11:59pm Pacific, 2:59am Eastern.
- Unfinished checklist items can be dismissed, so an action launches without them. No user requirement records this: this branch adds it without a recorded request, it waives the requirement that every readiness item be satisfied, and whether the requirement's author wants it is an open question for them. Dismissing an action's unfinished items dismisses each item then missing, one key per item, so a newly missing item blocks again while fixing one leaves the others dismissed. A dismissal is dropped once the page loads every check without error and finds its item fixed, so an item that goes missing after that blocks again; one fixed and broken again while the page is closed stays dismissed. A dismissal of an action taken out of the suite is dropped then too, so adding the action back blocks again. A load paused offline counts as still loading, so an action or task form whose load is paused keeps its dismissals. Restoring clears every dismissal of the action's items. While only some of a row's items are dismissed, those read "(dismissed)", so the newly missing one stands out. The row reads "Unfinished", not "Missing", since reading time over 15 minutes is an item too.
- Each action row shows its cohort's member count and description, an edit link for its task form, and notes to weigh before "Ready to launch": archived and an empty cohort. The notes don't block, because a cohort that depends on an earlier action can be empty at launch. A missing cover image isn't noted, at staff's request. The readiness checklist's "Image set" item, which `main` added after that request (#888), blocks launch for an action without a cover image until staff dismiss it; whether staff meant to launch without one, with no step to dismiss, is unrecorded, so this interaction of the two is open for Mark.
- An action that already has its own members taking action event (the action list's `memberActionStart`, which before launch can only come from the action's own events) is noted with that start and blocks the launch, since the server allows one live members-taking-action event per action and would refuse the launch after staff had confirmed it. Staff delete that event, or take the action out of the suite, first.
- Each action row shows the action editor's `OpenReferenceWarning`: a cohort that reads an action still open at launch, and not awaited as a prerequisite, freezes members' placement at launch, so someone who finishes the referenced action later never gets this one. It's evaluated against the launch schedule, with this suite's own actions given the office deadline the launch creates. Like the editor, it doesn't block.
- An action whose description and task form take longer to read than its time estimate gets a note, since member load and the 15-minute contract check sum time estimates. Reading time is the action dashboard's estimate (0.462 seconds a word), now `readingTime` in `actionReadiness.ts`. With variant forms, it's the longest read any member gets. The note doesn't block: an estimate can fairly assume skimming.
- A link members can't open is a text finding: an `http(s)://` link to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, or `0.0.0.0`, an `example.com`, `.org`, or `.net` placeholder domain or its subdomains, or an `admin.` or `staging.` host, where the host ends there (`localhostlabs.org` and `example.community` pass), as when a URL is copied from the admin panel or a test site. Only links with a scheme count, to keep false positives down; of the 70 local actions whose bodies have links, none is flagged.
- Placeholder text (TODO, TBD, TKTK, FIXME, lorem ipsum, `[insert …]`, `[add …]`, `[tbd …]`) is a text finding like the rest. The capitalized markers match only in capitals, so "your todo list" passes, and a bracket that is a markdown link's text, such as `[Add to calendar](…)`, isn't a placeholder, nor does a placeholder bracket run on past it.

## Form preview

Current behavior whose origin is unrecorded: no user requirement records it, so a later agent may reconsider any of it.

- The form builder's preview can preview the form as each kind of member its member conditions tell apart.
- Those member variants fold under a "Member variants" heading that shows how many variants there are.
- Only members with an active contract are grouped into variants.
- A variant card stays highlighted only while the preview shows the member that card picked.
- The suite launch page's "Task forms by member" lists the task forms whose conditions tell members apart, and a group opens the form previewed as one of its members.

## Task forms by member and A/B variant forms

- A reviewer, Charles Lien, left this choice to the agent. Each of an action's forms, its task form and each A/B variant form, is its own entry when its conditions tell members apart, previewed against that form's own schema and opening that form's preview. When the action has variants, its entries are labelled "task form" and 'variant "<name>"', each with its share of members (a variant's `splitValue`, the task form the remainder), read from the action's variants list, since a variant's form copies the task form's title. The section then says each member gets the task form or a variant at random, in the shares shown. Every entry groups the action's whole cohort, because a variant's members are drawn at random at assignment and can't be known before launch. Each entry's count reads "member variants", so it isn't mistaken for the A/B variants on the same line. For the same reason, a member in "Preview members' actions" gets a preview link for each of an action's forms, the task form and each variant by name, since which one they'll get isn't known before launch.
